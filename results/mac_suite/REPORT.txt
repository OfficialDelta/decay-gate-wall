What it means
The core hypothesis holds up: changing nothing but the decay-gate init removes the retrieval wall at L=64 (10/10 vs 1/10) and at L=256 (10/10 retain vs 0/5 baseline) with no curriculum, beating the paper's shaped-ramp curriculum (4/5) at L=256, and every lock-in is a genuine retriever. But B failed as pre-registered: the same init also solves collision decoys, which the "decoys overwrite rather than fade" story said it should not, so the mechanism account is incomplete. A likely but untested reason is that the cell is bidirectional and its backward stream naturally keeps the first (correct) binding once retention is the default.

Caveat on C: it passes as written, but the bias-6 vs bias-4 contrast it was designed around did not show up. Bias 4 also went 5/5 at L=256, contrary to the pre-registered estimate that it keeps only ~1% of the table after 246 tokens. So the simple per-token survival arithmetic underestimates what bias 4 can learn, and these runs do not show that bias 6 is needed at this length.
